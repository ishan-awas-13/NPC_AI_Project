Part 1: Project Overview & Core Research Question

Research Problem: In video games, state machines create rigid boundary snapping (e.g. violent flip-flopping at exactly 30% HP). This project investigates how continuous Fuzzy Logic affects NPC behavior, smoothness, combat outcomes, and computational costs compared to FSM, Behavior Trees, GOAP, and Utility AI.

An NPC (Non-Player Character) in this project features two decoupled systems:

  • Decision Brain: Evaluates environmental inputs (health, player distance, line-of-sight, whisker sensors) and outputs a desired velocity vector (V_desired).
  • Steering Body (Craig Reynolds Model): Calculates bounded steering force: $\vec{F}_{\text{steering}} = \text{clampLength}(\vec{V}_{\text{desired}} - \vec{V}_{\text{current}}, F_{\text{max}})$, creating smooth acceleration, natural cornering, and physical inertia.

Part 2: Complete Simulation Tick Lifecycle (src/game/GameEngine.ts)

The game loop runs via requestAnimationFrame. Every tick inside GameEngine.update(rawDt) follows this strict sequence:

  1. Delta Time & Clock: Clamps Δt ≤ 0.05s to prevent physics tunneling; starts profiler frame timer.
  2. Opponent Logic: In experiment mode, executes updateAutomatedPlayer() (deterministic kiting at 180px, automated EMP pulse shockwave at 135px).
  3. Whisker Raycasting: 5 ray sensors per NPC cast against crate bounding boxes (Math2D.rayIntersectAABB).
  4. AI Deliberation: Executes the selected architecture (Fuzzy, BT, FSM, GOAP, or Utility) and records microseconds.
  5. Flocking & Stamina: Applies inverse-square separation between NPCs to prevent stacking; depletes aggression stamina over 4s.
  6. Physics Integration: Velocity integration (V += F · Δt), speed clamping, and AABB crate collision clamping.
  7. Shrine & Combat Resolution: Heals NPCs inside shrines (25–30 HP/s); applies contact damage to player (22 HP/s).
  8. Trial Metrics Evaluation: In experiment mode, accumulates distance, debounces attacks, and finalizes trial on death or timeout.

Part 4: The Five AI Architectures: Deep In-Depth Analysis

4.1 Finite State Machine (FSM) / Simple Decision Logic (src/ai/simpleLogic.ts)

Core Mechanism: Hard Threshold If-Else Ladder

Evaluates discrete scalar checks:

if (health ≤ 30% || (isHealing && health < 75%)) → FLEE_TO_HEAL
else if (isExhausted) → EXHAUSTED_STANDOFF
else → DIRECT_CHASE

Obstacle Avoidance: Checks only the single center forward ray sensor. If hitDistance < 50px, it projects target direction onto the wall surface tangent (OBSTACLE_SLIDE).

Strengths & Weaknesses: Extremely low CPU load (2.8 μs). Weakness: Rigid behavior and sudden boundary snapping.

4.2 Behavior Trees (src/ai/behaviorTree.ts)

Core Mechanism: Hierarchical Selector & Sequence Traversal

Organized into a Root Priority Selector (evaluates high-level goals from left to right):

  • Branch 1 (Priority 1 - Survival Sequence): Is Health < 35%? → Navigate to Heal Station (boosts retreat speed by 1.25×).
  • Branch 2 (Priority 2 - Fatigue Sequence): Is Agent Exhausted? → Disengage & Catch Breath (maintains 180px standoff, 55% speed).
  • Branch 3 (Priority 3 - Cover Clearance Sequence): Player Behind Crate? (raycast check) → Navigate Around Crate Corner (evaluates all 4 crate corners).
  • Branch 4 (Fallback Action): Predictive Whisker Chase (5-ray whisker deflection with quadratic urgency).

Strengths & Weaknesses: Modular, readable, and highly maintainable (34.4 μs). Weakness: Still discrete; transitions can feel stepped.

4.3 Fuzzy Logic Controller (src/ai/fuzzy.ts)

Core Mechanism: Continuous Membership & Vector Weighted Centroid

1. Fuzzification Equations:
Triangular: trimf(x; a, b, c) = max(0, min((x - a) / (b - a), (c - x) / (c - b)))
Trapezoidal: trapmf(x; a, b, c, d) = max(0, min((x - a) / (b - a), 1, (d - x) / (d - c)))

2. The 5 Continuous Rules:

Rule 1: w_retreat = healthLow
Rule 2: w_flank = min(healthMed, distNear)
Rule 3: w_balanced = min(healthMed, max(distMed, distFar))
Rule 4: w_aggressive = min(healthHigh, distFar)
Rule 5: w_relentless = min(healthHigh, max(distNear, distMed))

3. Vector Defuzzification:
D_blended = Σ(w_i · k_i · V_i) / Σ(w_i · k_i) Where $k_{\text{retreat}}=1.6, k_{\text{flank}}=1.1, k_{\text{balanced}}=1.0, k_{\text{aggressive}}=1.3, k_{\text{relentless}}=1.4$.
Obstacle avoidance is continuously injected: D_final = normalize(D_blended + (1.4 + 1.5 · Danger_obstacle) · F_avoid).

Worked Example (HP=35%, Dist=150px):
healthLow = 0.50, healthMed = 0.20, distNear = 0.25, distMed = 0.40.
Weights: Retreat = 0.50 ($0.80$), Flank = 0.20 ($0.22$), Balanced = 0.20 ($0.20$). Total = 1.22.
Result: 65% Retreat, 18% Flank, 17% Chase. NPC angles away smoothly toward shrine without snapping!

4.4 Goal-Oriented Action Planning (GOAP) (src/ai/goap.ts)

Core Mechanism: A* Forward Search in World-State Space

World states are boolean atom dictionaries (e.g. { isHealthy, atHealingStation, inCombatRange, hasLineOfSight }).

  • Goals: Survive & Regenerate (Priority 95 if HP ≤ 35), Flank Obscured Player (Priority 70 if no LOS), Strike Player (Priority 50).
  • Actions Pool: Navigate to Healing Shrine, Regenerate HP in Shrine, Corner Flank Maneuver, Predictive Intercept Gap, Melee Pounce Attack.
  • Planner: Executes $A^*$ forward search finding the lowest-cost sequence whose chained preconditions and effects transform current world state into the desired goal state. Cost: $24.6\mu\text{s}$.

4.5 Utility AI (src/ai/utility.ts)

Core Mechanism: Mathematical Response Curves & Inertia Damping

Every action computes a continuous utility score U ∈ [0.0, 1.0]:

  • Heal Station Retreat (Exponential Curve): U = min(1.0, (1 - hpRatio)^2.2 × 1.4 × distFactor) × 1.25.
  • Corner Flank (Logistic Sigmoid Curve): $U = 0.82 \times \frac{1}{1 + e^{-6(d_{\text{norm}} - 0.3)}} \times \text{hpRatio}$.
  • Predictive Intercept (Linear Curve): $U = (0.45 + 0.35 \cdot d_{\text{norm}} + \text{motionBonus}) \times \text{hpRatio}$.
  • Melee Pounce (Quadratic Curve): U = (1 - d / 180)^2 × 0.95 × confidence × stamina × 1.15.
  • Inertia Hysteresis Bonus: Previously active action receives a +0.12 (12%) bonus to eliminate rapid high-frequency action flickering. Cost: $27.9\mu\text{s}$.

Part 7: Performance Metrics, Statistical Analysis & CSV Export

The platform collects 8 primary metrics in src/game/TrialMetricsCollector.ts:

  • Survival Time ($T_{\text{surv}}$): Seconds survived before death or timeout ($[0, T_{\text{limit}}]$).
  • Damage Dealt ($D_{\text{dealt}}$): Cumulative HP stripped from player (22 HP/s melee rate).
  • Damage Received ($D_{\text{recv}}$): Cumulative damage absorbed from player's EMP shockwave pulses (35 HP/blast).
  • Attacks / Retreats Count: Debounced melee contacts (0.4s cooldown) and edge-triggered retreat transitions.
  • Distance Travelled: Cumulative Euclidean path length ($\sum \sqrt{\Delta x^2 + \Delta y^2}$).
  • Sample Mean ($\bar{x}$) & Standard Deviation ($s$): s = √[ (1 / (N - 1)) · Σ(x_i - x̄)² ]. Measures behavioral consistency.

Data exports directly to standard RFC-4180 CSV via exportTrialsToCSV() for analysis in Python (Pandas/Matplotlib).

Part 8: Top 15 Viva Questions & Defensible Answers

1. What is the fundamental research question of your project?
"How does fuzzy-logic decision making affect NPC behavior compared with alternative AI decision architectures?" We investigate whether continuous degree-of-truth blending eliminates rigid threshold snapping and improves navigation fluidity in combat steering NPCs.
2. Why does the AI Profiler show Fuzzy Logic taking ~60.8μs compared to 2.8μs for Simple FSM?
FSM executes a single scalar if-else check and immediately exits (O(1)). Fuzzy Logic must compute continuous mathematical memberships across all sets, evaluate 5 continuous Mamdani rules, construct candidate geometric vectors, and compute a vector centroid normalization on every single frame.
3. Is defuzzification in your project Sugeno or Mamdani?
It is Mamdani-style continuous inference using Vector Weighted Centroid defuzzification. Rule firing strengths scale continuous directional vectors, and their normalized weighted vector sum forms the final desired velocity.
4. How does your system ensure fair benchmarking between the 5 AI architectures?
In experiment mode, human player input is replaced by a deterministic automated bot executing exact kiting and EMP pulse defenses. All NPCs share identical physics, max speeds, ray sensors, deterministic seeds, and spawn coordinates across 5 standardized scenario maps.
5. What is the role of Craig Reynolds' Steering Model in your architecture?
It decouples cognitive decision-making from physics. The AI brain only outputs a desired velocity (V_desired). Reynolds steering calculates $\vec{F} = \vec{V}_{\text{desired}} - \vec{V}_{\text{actual}}$, applying physical acceleration, mass, and cornering momentum.
6. How does Utility AI avoid violent toggling between actions with close scores?
Through inertia hysteresis (src/ai/utility.ts: L190-L218). The currently active action receives a +0.12 (12%) inertia bonus, requiring competing actions to be significantly more useful before a switch is triggered.
7. How does GOAP handle dynamic obstacle occlusion?
GOAP checks hasLineOfSight atom on every tick. If an obstacle blocks line of sight, the Corner Flank Maneuver action becomes eligible, calculating pathing around the obstacle's closest corner.
8. What is the difference between a Selector and a Sequence in a Behavior Tree?
A Selector implements logical OR (ticks children until one returns SUCCESS or RUNNING). A Sequence implements logical AND (ticks children until one returns FAILURE or RUNNING).
9. Why is high damage dealt alone insufficient to claim an AI is "better"?
An NPC could suicidal-rush the player and inflict high damage before dying in 3 seconds. True AI competence requires balancing damage output with survival time, health management, and retreat execution.
10. What does the sample standard deviation (s) in your CSV metrics represent?
It measures behavioral variance across seeds. Low standard deviation indicates deterministic stability; high standard deviation indicates sensitivity to geometry or spatial trapping.
11. What is the Aggression Stamina system?
When sprinting or in close-range aggressive pursuit, stamina drains over 4 seconds. When empty, isExhausted drops top speed to 55% for ~5 seconds, giving the player an escape window and creating tactical pacing.
12. How does whisker obstacle avoidance work mathematically?
5 ray sensors cast at 0°, ±24°, ±49°. Repulsive normal force scales quadratically: $F_{\text{avoid}} \propto (1 - d/L)^2$, creating subtle nudges at long distance and emergency evasions at close distance.
13. What is the main limitation of your Fuzzy Logic implementation?
Membership parameters (the $a,b,c,d$ values in `trimf`/`trapmf`) are statically hand-tuned. In future work, these could be auto-optimized using Genetic Algorithms or Adaptive Neuro-Fuzzy Inference Systems (ANFIS).
14. Why use TypeScript and HTML5 Canvas instead of Unity or Unreal?
Zero engine bloat, 100% transparent algorithmic code, and immediate cross-platform execution in any web browser without binary installation. We have direct mathematical control over every vector, raycast, and microsecond timer.
15. What is your 2-minute elevator pitch conclusion?
Fuzzy Logic costs 21× more CPU than simple FSM (60 μs vs 2.8 μs), but both comfortably fit within the 16.6ms 60 FPS frame budget (<0.5%). In exchange for this negligible microsecond cost, Fuzzy Logic completely eliminates threshold jitter and delivers superior, organic multi-goal navigation.
